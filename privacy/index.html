Galactic Getaway privacy policy

Effective: October 24, 2026

Address: https://crowbotlabs.com/privacy

Who we are

Galactic Getaway is made by Crowbot Labs. “We” means Crowbot Labs. Write to us at privacy@crowbotlabs.com.

The game has no accounts and no sign-in, and we run no servers. Your progress is saved on your iPhone. The services the game uses, mostly Unity’s, collect the data below, and we see it through their dashboards.

What the game collects, and who collects it

At first launch, before the ad services or Unity Analytics start, the “Your privacy” screen asks about Personalized ads and Analytics. (Crash reporting, below, runs from the moment the game opens.) Both start off. “Save my choices” keeps what you set; “Accept all” turns both on. Your answer is stored on this iPhone, not in your game save, and the game does not send it to us. The ad services and Unity In-App Purchasing are told it (below).

Ads: Unity LevelPlay and Unity Ads

The game shows rewarded ads only: an ad plays only when you choose to watch one for a reward. Unity LevelPlay (formerly ironSource) picks the ad network. While the game is in testing on TestFlight, it runs on LevelPlay’s demo setup instead of our ad account. Unity runs that setup and chooses its ad networks, and we earn nothing from its ads. We don’t know which networks it uses; each collects data under its own privacy policy. On our own ad account, LevelPlay uses only Unity Ads.

Unity says its ad services collect a device ID, a user ID, your general location (such as your country), how you use the game and its ads, purchase history, performance data (such as start-up speed and energy use), and device details such as language, model, screen size and connection type. If you report an ad, they may collect the report.

  • Personalized ads on: ads can be picked using data about you and this iPhone.
  • Off: ads still play. Unity says they are then matched to the game, not to you, and don’t rely on personal identifiers. The ad services still run and receive device data. Unity does not say how much less they collect.

Each time the game starts, it tells the ad services your answer. With personalized ads off, it also tells them you have opted out of the sale or sharing of your data. It always tells them not to treat you as a child.

Inside any Unity ad, the Data Privacy icon lets you opt out of certain personalized ads and, Unity says, see the data it has about you in this game.

The game also contains Google’s ad code (AdMob), but never initializes it and shows no Google ads. Google does not say whether that code collects anything when not initialized.

Apple’s tracking prompt

At first launch, if you allow personalized ads, Apple’s tracking prompt appears before ads and analytics start, unless you have answered it before or turned off “Allow Apps to Request to Track”. If you leave personalized ads off, the game never shows it.

If you do not allow tracking, apps cannot use your iPhone’s advertising ID. Apple says it “will be all zeros”. Change this any time in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

Analytics: Unity Analytics, only if you turn it on

Off: the game drops its own analytics events, and Unity Analytics records nothing.

On: Unity Analytics collects your IP address, an ID made for this installation of the game, and the IDFV (an ID Apple gives our apps on your iPhone). It records device details: model, iOS version, chip, graphics, memory, screen size, language and region, battery level and volume. It logs when sessions start and end, and checks in about once a minute while you play. Unity In-App Purchasing adds each purchase and failed purchase. The game adds six events of its own:

  1. a new deepest wave: the wave number;
  2. a boss fight: which boss, whether you won, and how long it took;
  3. a Dimension Jump: how many you have made, and the payout’s size;
  4. a rewarded ad watched: which reward it was for;
  5. a return after at least a minute away: how long, and how many waves the game cleared meanwhile;
  6. an upgrade bought: which one, its new level, and which shop.

We use these to balance the game.

Crash reports: Unity Diagnostics, always

Crash reports are sent whatever you answer; the game has no switch for them. When the game hits an error or crashes, Unity Diagnostics receives the error, the events leading up to it, device details (model, iOS version, chip and graphics), the Unity version, and IDs for this installation and session. Reports may include the game’s log messages. Diagnostics also records each play session, so we can see how often the game crashes.

The “Remove Ads” purchase: Apple and Unity In-App Purchasing

Apple takes the payment; the game never sees your payment details. Unity In-App Purchasing connects to Apple’s store when the game opens, before you answer the privacy screen, and passes the purchase to the game. Whatever your Analytics answer, it also sends Unity:

  • a record at each step of a purchase (started, paid, failed, completed): the product’s name and price, Apple’s transaction ID, device details (model, iOS version, language and region settings, storage size, last restart), installation, session and Analytics IDs, the IDFV, app and Unity versions, the install date, and your two privacy answers. Once paid, it adds Apple’s signed record of the purchase and the game’s App Store receipt; for a failure, the reason. It adds the advertising ID only if you allowed personalized ads and Unity’s settings for the game ask for it. Without tracking permission, that ID is all zeros.
  • a settings request each time the game starts, with the project and installation IDs, the platform and version details and, like any request, your IP address.

Unity says it handles this data for us, and also uses transaction and billing data for its own purposes, such as financial reporting, legal obligations and fraud prevention. Unity In-App Purchasing has no opt-out, and the game does not add one.

TestFlight

If you test the game through Apple’s TestFlight, Apple collects crash logs and usage information, and you cannot opt out. Apple shares with us your name and email (if we invited you by email); install, session and crash counts; your device model and identifier, iOS version and language; crash logs; and any feedback or screenshot you send, with details such as carrier, time zone, battery level and free storage.

We use this to find and fix bugs and improve the game. In TestFlight, purchases run in Apple’s sandbox, which Apple says takes no real payment.

Your choices

  • Change your answers any time: tap the gear, then Privacy choices. It reopens the same screen, with Cancel.
    • The game’s own events stop or start at once, and so does Unity Analytics. When it stops, it first tries to send what it has already recorded.
    • The ad services are told at once, and again each time the game starts.
    • Turning personalized ads on can bring up Apple’s tracking prompt, if never answered.
    • Turning them off does not change Apple’s tracking setting. Change that in Settings.
    • Turning Analytics off does not stop crash reports or the purchase records.
  • Read this policy in the game: tap the gear, then Privacy policy.
  • Your answer belongs to this iPhone. Another device asks again. Deleting the game deletes your answer, so reinstalling asks again. Restoring your iPhone from a backup may bring it back.

Children

Galactic Getaway is not made for children. It does not ask your age. If you believe a child has played it and want their data deleted, write to us and we will ask the services to delete it.

How long data is kept

As each provider states it:

  • Unity Analytics: 13 months by default.
  • Unity Diagnostics: 90 days by default.
  • Unity In-App Purchasing: service logs for 90 days; order records until we or you ask for them to be deleted.
  • Unity Ads and LevelPlay: Unity gives no set period. It keeps personal data “for as long as is necessary or as permitted in light of the purpose(s) for which it was obtained”.
  • Apple TestFlight: feedback for one year; crash logs and usage data may be kept until the bugs are resolved.
  • On your iPhone: until you delete the game.

Your rights, and how to reach us

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct or delete data about you, or to object to its use.

  • Write to us: privacy@crowbotlabs.com. We keep little ourselves: testers’ names and emails, copies of tester feedback and crash details in our development notes, and emails you send us. For data the services hold for us, we will ask them on your behalf. For data a service holds for its own purposes, write to it directly (below). The game has no account or player ID to show you, so a service may not be able to find your data. We will tell you what we could do.
  • Unity: DPO@unity3d.com. For purchase records Unity uses for its own purposes: unity-iap-contact@unity3d.com.
  • Inside a Unity ad: the Data Privacy icon.
  • Apple: privacy.apple.com, for data Apple holds. For your TestFlight data, write to us.

Changes to this policy

We will post any change here, with a new effective date. Before the game adds an ad network or another data service, we will update this policy.
